**Project Athena**, developed at MIT, was a distributed computing environment that allowed your personal files and settings to follow you from one Athena workstation to another. Its technologies included Kerberos, Hesiod, Ingres, Moira, Zephyr, the Andrew File System (AFS), and the X Window System.

![Historical Project Athena computer lab at MIT]({{ '/images/athena/athena-computer-lab.jpg' | relative_url }})

*Project Athena workstations at MIT. Photo courtesy of the MIT Museum, via [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2018/mit-looking-back-project-athena-distributed-computing-for-students-1111).*

I had the good fortune to get an account in this environment while working in the Distributed Computing Group at UC Santa Cruz. As an end user, I experienced the tail end of one of the many historic developments in computing.

![Project Athena system architecture, showing applications, shared services, file systems, and network layers]({{ '/images/athena/athena-system-architecture-1992.png' | relative_url }})

*Project Athena system architecture. Figure 2 from J. M. Arfman and P. Roden, “Project Athena: Supporting distributed computing at MIT,” IBM Systems Journal, volume 31, number 3, 1992, page 555. The figure was redrawn with MIT’s permission, according to its original credit. Scan via [Bitsavers](https://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/ibm/IBM_Systems_Journal/313/ibmsj3103I.pdf).*

The original Project Athena partnership ended in 1991, but the computing environment continued to evolve. I’ve come across two later projects: [Debathena](https://debathena.mit.edu/), which brought Athena to Debian and Ubuntu, and [Nixathena](https://forgejo.mit.edu/SIPB/nixathena), which brings Athena to modern NixOS. Nixathena even preserves the original Debathena login screen while running it on Wayland using labwc—an interesting connection between Athena’s history and the move beyond the X Window System.

![MIT students using Athena workstations to visualize electric fields in 1984]({{ '/images/athena/athena-electric-fields-1984.jpg' | relative_url }})

*MIT electrical engineering and computer science students using Athena workstations to visualize electric fields, 1984. Photo courtesy of the MIT Infinite History Project, via [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2018/mit-looking-back-project-athena-distributed-computing-for-students-1111).*

Here are some articles and a book I found for further reading:

- **[Looking back at Project Athena](https://news.mit.edu/2018/mit-looking-back-project-athena-distributed-computing-for-students-1111) — November 11, 2018.** An MIT News retrospective on how Athena brought computing into students’ everyday education, its partnership with IBM and DEC, and its lasting influence.

- **[Project Athena: Success in Engineering Projects](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-933j-the-structure-of-engineering-revolutions-fall-2001/4e5bb2049bf13545ff6f308b9ca90538_final.pdf) — Fall 1999.** A student research paper examining Athena’s educational goals, technical achievements, and the competing priorities of MIT faculty, IBM, and DEC.

- **[Project Athena: Supporting distributed computing at MIT](https://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/ibm/IBM_Systems_Journal/313/ibmsj3103I.pdf) — 1992, IBM Systems Journal, volume 31, number 3.** A technical overview by J. M. Arfman and P. Roden explaining Athena’s architecture, network services, and administration, including how users could access their files and applications from different workstations.

- **[MIT Project Athena: A Model for Distributed Campus Computing](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/mit-project-athena/luisa/978-1-55558-072-8) — George A. Champine, 1991, Digital Press.** A book-length account of Athena’s design, implementation, and operation, drawing on project reports and the author’s firsthand experience. [Read the scanned book (PDF)]({{ '/files/MIT_Project_Athena_1991.pdf' | relative_url }}), originally downloaded from [Bitsavers](https://bitsavers.trailing-edge.com/pdf/mit/projectAthena/MIT_Project_Athena_A_Model_For_Distributed_Campus_Computing_1991.pdf).
